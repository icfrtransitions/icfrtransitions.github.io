ICFR After an Acquisition: What the One-Year Exclusion Allows

SEC staff guidance in plain terms: when a newly acquired business may be left out of management's assessment of internal control over financial reporting (ICFR), and what that does not excuse.

What the SEC staff guidance says

SEC staff guidance on management's ICFR report (Question 3) and the SEC's Financial Reporting Manual, section 4310.11 cover a business acquired during the year:

  • A condition, not an election. The staff do not object to an exclusion when it was not possible to assess the acquired business's ICFR between the acquisition date and the date of management's assessment.
  • Two limits. The exclusion may not extend beyond one year from the acquisition date, and the business may not be left out of more than one annual management report.
  • Disclosure. Management must identify the excluded business and state its significance to the consolidated financial statements.
  • Changes still reported. Any material change in ICFR caused by the acquisition must still be disclosed.
  • Reverse acquisitions. A similar exclusion can apply after certain reverse acquisitions with a private operating company (FRM 4310.11, item 4, referring to Regulation S-K C&DI 215.02).

Not a one-year holiday. The exclusion limits what management assesses in one report. It does not relieve the company of maintaining controls over the acquired business, whose results remain in the consolidated financial statements. Where an auditor's ICFR opinion is required, the auditor may limit its audit in the same way. It must also evaluate whether the exclusion and its disclosure are reasonable (PCAOB AS 2201, B14–B16).

Four common misreadings

  1. "Excluded" is not "ineffective." An exclusion describes the scope of the assessment, not a conclusion about the acquired business's controls.
  2. The clock runs from the acquisition date. Track the one-year limit separately from fiscal year-end and filing dates.
  3. Later disclosures need care. A weakness disclosed after the first full assessment is recorded as a later event. Whether it also says something about the earlier period depends on the later filing, for example if it identifies a weakness that already existed or revises an earlier conclusion.
  4. Company-wide weaknesses are not automatically acquisition-related. Treat them as acquisition-related only when the disclosure links them.

Planning back from the first included year-end

  1. Record the acquisition date and the first annual assessment at which the business must be included.
  2. Inventory the acquired business's significant processes and systems.
  3. Assign control owners, and align close calendars, reconciliations and system access.
  4. Leave enough time for controls to operate, and be tested, before that year-end.
  5. Track deficiencies and remediation through to tested operating effectiveness.
