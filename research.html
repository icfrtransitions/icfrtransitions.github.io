ICFR Transition Readiness: The Research Program

How public companies move acquired businesses from "excluded" to "assessed", and how newly public companies reach their first management assessment, studied from SEC filings.

The questions

When a company excludes a newly acquired business from management's ICFR assessment, what does it disclose? What changes at the next annual assessment, and what do later filings show about integration and remediation? And when a newly public company omits management's report under the SEC transition period, what does its first assessment show?

Method in brief

  • Sources: Form 10-K Item 9A, management's and auditors' reports, and acquisition disclosures, each cited with its filing.
  • Records: linked records for each company-year, each excluded business and each disclosed weakness, followed to the next annual report.
  • Observed versus inferred: what a filing says is recorded separately from what is inferred. An exclusion is never treated as a weakness. A company-wide weakness is not attributed to an acquisition unless the filing links them.
  • Checks: every case is verified by hand against its filing. An independent second coder checks a subset. Guidance is released only after independent technical review and usability testing.

The full method is set out in research protocol v1.4, registered publicly on OSF on October 31, 2026, before the main analysis: osf.io/jnpr2 (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/JNPR2).

History

The research moved here on October 31, 2026, as the independent, non-commercial ICFR Transitions Project. That earlier note referred to "five pilot cases" with baseline assessments from 2023 to 2025. Those cases could not be traced to the project's records. They are superseded by the documented pilot described above and are not part of the study's data.
